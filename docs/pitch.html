The opening

0:00 · 20s

Every AI tool will make you a launch video in seconds now. Nobody checks them.

The logo flashes by in under two seconds. The text is yellow on white, so you can't read it on a phone. The colours are a little off brand. And a human finds that out after it's posted.

Liftoff doesn't let a video out until it can prove it's done.

Start with the problem, not the product. Let "nobody checks them" land before you move on.

What it is

0:20 · 15s

Liftoff is a team of AI agents. You give it a brand's website. It writes down what "done" means for that launch, makes three videos, and has a separate checker measure every one. If a video misses, it goes back with exactly what failed. And it learns from its own misses.

Why you can trust it

2:05 · 30s

Three rules make the checking honest, and they're enforced in code, not asked for in a prompt.

  1. The checker never sees the maker's notes. It can't be told "the logo is on for 3.4 seconds". It has to measure.
  2. The maker and the checker are different model families. DeepSeek makes, NVIDIA Nemotron checks, so they don't share blind spots.
  3. A final reviewer sees only your original ask and three frames. Never the checklist. That catches a video that passes every check but misses the point.

This is the "Done Means Done" part. Say it plainly.

It learns

2:35 · 20s

Scroll to "It checks itself, and it learns".

After each launch it looks at what failed and writes itself rules. But a rule stays only if it helps on launch briefs it has never seen. We tested on held-out briefs: first-try passes went from 0% to 50%. A second round of rules didn't help, so it threw them away.

The technical parts

If they ask, or you have time.

The flow
Brand card, then research, then the Spec agent writes the checklist, then the Director writes three directions, they're rendered, the verifier checks each, the Arbiter decides GO, HOLD, SCRUB or ask a human, and a goal re-check gives the final yes.
The checker is mostly maths, not vibes
Pixel checks run in Python: logo time by template matching on sampled frames, WCAG contrast measured on the actual text pixels, colour distance from the brand palette, duration, aspect ratio. Only things that need eyes, like "is there a hook in the first second", go to a vision model, one frame and one yes or no question at a time.
The fix loop
On a HOLD, the Arbiter hands the Director exactly what failed with the measured number. The Director can only move real levers, like logo time, background colour or the footage, so every fix maps to something the checker measures. Up to three tries, then it's dropped.
Learning (Version N+1)
20 missions: 10 to train on, 10 held out. A playbook agent reads every miss and writes at most six rules per role. They're kept only if the held-out first-try pass rate goes up and the overall pass rate doesn't go down.
Speed
Checks run in parallel with a shared limit, and retry with backoff on rate limits. A full launch, with research, checklist, three videos, every check, retries and the final review, takes about two minutes.
The videos
The pipeline renders with Pillow and ffmpeg so every lever is measurable. The poppi showcase videos were animated in Remotion from the ideas Liftoff wrote, then held to exactly the same 10 checks. All three passed.
Runs offline
LIFTOFF_MOCK=1 runs the whole thing end to end with no keys, for testing.

How each sponsor is used

"Every model call runs on Crusoe. Nemotron is the checker. You.com does the research."

Crusoe Managed Inference

Every model call goes through Crusoe, behind one wrapper (liftoff/llm.py) that maps each agent role to a model. Having Nemotron, DeepSeek, GLM and Qwen on one key let us run a real 8-model bake-off to pick the video writer, and keep maker and checker in different families. Turning thinking off for the checklist call cut it from 56 seconds to about 25.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3

  • Super 120B writes the checklist for each launch, and the rules the system learns from its failures.
  • Nano 30B reads the brand's website into a brand card and tags what's working in viral videos.
  • Nano Omni is the checker's eyes: hook, end card, competitor logos and health claims on sampled frames. It's also the final reviewer, since it takes three frames in one prompt.

You.com

Contents reads the brand's own website, so brand colours come from the site's real CSS and are never made up. Search finds what's happening in the category and backs the checklist's thresholds with sources.

Also used

YouTube Data API for real viral Shorts and view counts, with no scraping. ElevenLabs for the demo video's voiceover. Remotion for the poppi showcase videos and the demo video.

Questions you'll probably get

Isn't this just an AI checking an AI?

Mostly not. Logo time, contrast, colour, length and shape are measured in code. The model only answers narrow yes or no questions about single frames, and it's a different family from the one that made the video.

Why not use a video generation model?

Because "done" has to be fixable. If the checker says the logo was on for 2 seconds and needs 3, our maker can change exactly that. A generative model would just roll the dice again.

How do you know the learning is real?

It's scored on briefs it never trained on, and a rule that doesn't improve that score gets thrown away. One round helped, 0 to 50%. The next didn't, so it was reverted.

What would you build next?

Real footage from stock libraries, posting straight to TikTok and Instagram after approval, and running the agents in a locked-down sandbox on Crusoe Cloud.

What's the human's role?

Approve or skip. It only asks you something mid-launch when a check can't be measured, and for poppi that was zero times.
